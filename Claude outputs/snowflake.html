Star vs. snowflake vs. constellation

the difference is what gets split apart, not how many tables there are
1. Star schema — dimensions stay flat (your current design)
Every dimension is one flat table pointing at the fact. dim_league has country baked right in as a plain column — no separate country table. Simple to query: one join gets you everything about a league.
2. Snowflake — a dimension gets split apart
dim_league no longer holds country directly — it points at a separate dim_country table instead. Now getting "which country is this league in" costs an extra join (fact_match → dim_league → dim_country). This is snowflaking: normalizing a dimension into multiple linked tables. Star schemas deliberately avoid this — the redundancy of repeating "England" on every English league row is considered a fair trade for not needing the extra join.
3. Constellation / galaxy — a second fact table, sharing dimensions (what you sketched)
Two fact tables at different grains — fact_match (one row per match) and fact_league_season (one row per league-season, holding the aggregated match_count/total_goals) — both pointing at the same shared, still-flat dim_league and dim_season. No dimension got split apart, so this is still built from stars, just two of them sharing points. This is the pattern for your goals/matches idea.
